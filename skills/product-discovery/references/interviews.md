# Interviews and evidence gathering

## Mom Test rules (non-negotiable)

1. Talk about their life, not your idea.
2. Ask about past behavior ("tell me about the last time you…"), never about hypotheticals ("would you use…?").
3. Listen more than you talk — you speak <20% of the time.
4. Opinions, compliments, and promises ("I'd definitely pay") count as zero evidence. Commitments and stories count.

## Question bank

**Problem exploration**
- "Walk me through the last time you dealt with <problem area>."
- "What did you do right before that? And after?"
- "What's the hardest part of <process>? Why?"
- "How often does that happen?"
- "What have you already tried to fix it? What happened?"

**Current-state evidence**
- "What tools/spreadsheets do you use today for this?"
- "How much time does it take? Who else is involved?"
- "Where does it break, and what does it cost you when it does?"

**Validation (only after the problem is confirmed)**
- "Who else should I talk to about this?"

## Anti-patterns

- Pitching the solution mid-interview (poisons all later answers).
- Leading questions: "Don't you find it frustrating when…?"
- Collecting feature requests instead of problem evidence.
- Interviewing colleagues or friends who won't be users.
- Stopping at 2 interviews — 5 is the minimum for a pattern; keep going while each interview still yields surprises.

## Non-interview evidence

| Source | What it proves | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Product analytics (funnels, cohorts) | what users actually do | strong |
| Support tickets / sales objections | recurring pain, verbatim | strong |
| Churn/exit surveys | why users leave | strong |
- Competitor reviews (G2/Capterra 1–3★) | unmet needs in the market | medium |
- Community/forum threads | pain in the wild | medium |
- Internal SMEs | domain constraints | weak for demand, strong for constraints |

Triangulate: a problem confirmed by interviews AND analytics is ready for the PRD; a problem confirmed by only one source needs one more experiment.
